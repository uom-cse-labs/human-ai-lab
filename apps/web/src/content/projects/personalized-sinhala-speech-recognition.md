Voice assistants don't work for people whose speech is hard to understand. For Sinhala speakers, there isn't even a training dataset that tries. This project builds a speech-recognition system for Sinhala-speaking individuals with speech impairments such as dysarthria, built around 40 everyday phrases spanning needs like food, pain, help, and emergencies.

Rather than relying on a generic model, it learns each user's own voice from a handful of enrolled recordings and matches new attempts against that personal profile, combined with a general speech-to-text signal, to produce one confident answer or a short list of options when it isn't sure.

The system is implemented end-to-end, and a small pilot study with real users is the current next step.
